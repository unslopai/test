## § 1 Scope and precedence

(1) This DPA applies to all personal data the Processor processes on behalf of the Controller when the Controller uses the unslop service (GitHub App, CLI, VS Code extension, MCP server, dashboard). It supplements the unslop Terms of Service (the "Main Agreement").

(2) The Controller concludes this DPA electronically by agreeing to it at sign-up or in the dashboard (Art. 28(9) GDPR). The Processor records the account, time and version of the agreement and provides this DPA as a PDF. On request, the parties also sign it. (3) In case of conflict, this DPA takes precedence over the Main Agreement.

(4) For data the Processor processes for its own purposes (such as the Controller's account and billing data), it is itself the controller; its Privacy Policy applies to that data, not this DPA.

## § 2 Subject matter, nature, purpose and duration

(1) The subject matter, nature and purpose of the processing, the types of personal data and the categories of data subjects are set out in Annex 2.

(2) This DPA applies for as long as the Main Agreement exists and beyond that until the Processor has deleted or returned all data under § 14.

## § 3 Instructions

(1) The Processor processes personal data only on documented instructions from the Controller, including with regard to transfers to third countries. The instructions are this DPA with its annexes and the settings the Controller makes in the service (such as which repositories it connects). The Controller gives further instructions in text form to the contact address in Annex 1.

(2) If the Processor is required to process data by Union or Member State law, it informs the Controller of that legal requirement before processing, unless that law prohibits such information on important grounds of public interest.

(3) If the Processor considers that an instruction infringes the GDPR or other data protection provisions, it informs the Controller without undue delay. It may suspend execution until the Controller confirms or changes the instruction.

(4) Instructions that go beyond the scope of services of the Main Agreement are treated as a request to change the services.

## § 4 Purpose limitation

The Processor processes the data exclusively to provide the service to the Controller, including error analysis of individual requests and the security of the service. It does not use the data for its own purposes, in particular not for training AI models, for benchmarks or for improving review rules, unless the Controller has expressly agreed to this in text form in the individual case. ## § 5 Confidentiality

(1) Only the Processor's managing director has access to the data and is bound to confidentiality.

(2) Before further persons receive access, the Processor commits them to confidentiality in text form, unless they are under an appropriate statutory obligation of confidentiality, and instructs them in data protection (Art. 28(3)(b), Art. 29 GDPR).

## § 6 Technical and organizational measures

(1) The Processor takes the measures under Art. 32 GDPR described in Annex 3.

(2) It may adapt the measures to technical progress provided the level of protection does not decrease. It notifies the Controller of material changes in advance in text form.

(3) It reviews the measures at least once a year and when there is cause (new sub-processor, security incident, significant architectural change) and documents the result.

## § 7 Sub-processors

(1) The Controller gives general authorization for the sub-processors listed in Annex 4. The Processor selects new sub-processors only if they (a) are based in the EU or EEA, or an adequacy decision or EU Standard Contractual Clauses cover the transfer, (b) offer a contract under Art. 28(4) GDPR, and (c) demonstrate a recognized security certification (such as ISO/IEC 27001 or SOC 2 Type 2) or equivalent documented measures.

(2) The Processor informs the Controller by email to the address stored in Annex 1 at least 30 days before a new or replacement sub-processor processes data. If it objects, the parties seek a solution within 14 days. If none is found, the Controller may terminate the Main Agreement for cause; section 11.4 of the Terms of Service applies.

(4) If a sub-processor poses an acute security risk or fails, the Processor may suspend the affected processing. It uses a replacement only if the replacement is already authorized as a backup in Annex 4 or the procedure under paragraphs 2 and 3 has been completed. It informs the Controller of the suspension without undue delay.

(5) If a sub-processor changes its own sub-processors, the Processor passes the change on to the Controller as soon as it learns of it. It has no influence on those timelines. (1) Transfers to third countries take place only as described for the respective sub-processor in Annex 4, on the basis stated there (adequacy decision under Art. 45 GDPR or EU Standard Contractual Clauses under Art. 46(2)(c) GDPR, Module 3 for transfers between processors).

## Annex 2: Description of the processing

**Categories of data subjects:** persons whose data is contained in the Controller's code, such as developers, persons named in comments or configuration, and persons in test data; persons who contribute to the Controller's repositories, insofar as their data appears in pull request metadata, review comments or reasoning for findings.

**Types of personal data:** personal data contained in the Controller's source code, diffs, configuration and test data (such as names, email addresses, user IDs); GitHub usernames in repository and account names; reasoning with which users mark findings as resolved. Special categories of personal data (Art. 9 GDPR) are not the subject of the processing. The Controller should not include them in reviewed code.

**Nature and purpose of the processing:** automated review of source code (code review) on behalf of the Controller:

1. fetching diffs, changed files and individual related configuration files through the GitHub API after installation of the GitHub App or through a repository webhook that the Processor creates with the Controller's GitHub access token, or receiving diffs from the CLI, VS Code extension and MCP server;
2. review with fixed rules and matching of package and model names against public registries (only the names as they appear in the code; they may contain a package author's account name);
3. transmission to AI models at Google Cloud Vertex AI for analysis and verification, and of a diff excerpt (file names and up to 30 added lines) to generate a search vector that is not stored;
4. storage of the request, result and log data, including in the hosting provider's runtime logs;
5. writing results back as check runs (for a repository webhook instead as a review under the user's GitHub account that requests changes or, on the user's own pull requests, only comments), review comments and, where needed, a notice comment to the Controller's GitHub repository; reading existing pull request comments only to avoid duplicate findings, and our own review comments to be able to resolve findings later; resolving the discussion of a finding marked as resolved;
6. for project context: reading the file list, individual manifest files (only the detected ecosystems are stored) and the JavaScript and TypeScript files, storing essentially their structure (signatures, interfaces, type definitions, class outlines; the automatic selection can include individual further lines of code), converting it into search vectors and storing it.

## Annex 3: Technical and organizational measures

**Preliminary note.** The Processor is operated by one person. It operates no data centers or servers of its own; processing takes place at the sub-processors under Annex 4. Physical security, hardware encryption, redundancy and data center operations are measures of these providers and are evidenced by their certifications (no. 7). The Processor is itself responsible for the following measures. It has no certification of its own.

**1. Encryption and pseudonymization (Art. 32(1)(a))**
- All connections between users, the service, GitHub, Google Cloud and Supabase are TLS-encrypted.
- Stored data is encrypted by the sub-processors (Google, Supabase and Vercel each use AES-256).
- The service additionally stores GitHub access tokens encrypted (AES-256-GCM; the key is stored like the other service credentials under no. 2).
- The service stores API keys only as a SHA-256 hash and a short prefix for recognition.
- Code is not pseudonymized, because the review requires the plain text.

**2. Confidentiality (Art. 32(1)(b))**
- Only the managing director has access to the admin consoles of Google Cloud, Supabase, Vercel, GitHub and Paddle, each with two-factor authentication. [BESTÄTIGEN: 2FA auf allen genannten Konten aktiv]
- Service credentials (database service key, Google service account, GitHub App private key, token encryption key) are stored as environment variables at the hosting provider and, for maintenance and error analysis, in an unversioned file on the managing director's encrypted workstation; not in the source code.
- Tenant separation: database queries run server-side and are restricted to the customer's account or installation. Browser clients have no access to the tables containing processed data (privileges revoked by database migration; an integration test against the database verifies this when run with credentials); the only exception is read access to one's own repository entries, limited by row level security.
- Incoming webhooks from GitHub and Paddle are verified by signature.
- Admin functions in the dashboard are restricted to a fixed list of operator accounts.
- Workstation: disk encryption, screen lock, current security updates; processed data is not stored permanently on the workstation. [BESTÄTIGEN V17] [VORBEDINGUNG V21: keine Inhalte von Auftragsdaten in KI-gestützten Arbeitssitzungen, über alle Wege (db-query-Skript, Supabase-MCP, Vercel-Laufzeitprotokolle); Abfragen auf Kennungen, Status, Zeitstempel und Zähler beschränkt]
- The CLI, the MCP server and the VS Code extension (through the CLI) abort the transmission if they detect access keys or private keys in the diff. For code fetched by the GitHub App there is no such server-side filtering.

**3. Integrity (Art. 32(1)(b))**
- Changes to the service are checked locally with automated tests before deployment and brought in through pull requests; deployments are traceable in the hosting provider's version history. [BESTÄTIGEN: Branch-Schutz auf main bzw. PR-Pflicht, alternativ Satz auf den Ist-Stand kürzen]
- The status of every review request is logged in the database; the hosting provider makes runtime logs visible for one day. [VORBEDINGUNG V1]

**4. Availability and resilience (Art. 32(1)(b))**
- Hosting at Vercel (function region Frankfurt) [VORBEDINGUNG V1], database at Supabase (Frankfurt (eu-central-1)); availability follows their infrastructure.
- The Processor gives no availability commitment of its own unless separately agreed.

**5. Restorability (Art. 32(1)(c))**
- [SICHERUNGSKOPIEN: je nach Supabase-Tarif "daily backups kept for 7 days by Supabase" oder "no automatic backups; restoration by fetching again"]
- The application code is versioned in a Git repository and can be redeployed.
- Code from GitHub repositories can be restored by fetching it again.

**6. Regular review (Art. 32(1)(d))**
- Review of these measures at least annually and when there is cause, with documented results.
- Annual review of the sub-processors' contracts and certificates.

**7. Certifications of the sub-processors**
- Google Cloud (Generative AI on Vertex AI): ISO/IEC 27001, 27017, 27018; SOC 1, 2, 3
- Vercel: SOC 2 Type 2; ISO/IEC 27001:2022
- Supabase: SOC 2 Type 2; ISO/IEC 27001

**8. Data minimization and AI-specific measures**

Titles, descriptions and author details of pull requests are not evaluated and not sent to AI models.
- Google does not train models with the data (Google Cloud Service Specific Terms, "Training Restriction").
- The context cache the service creates at Google contains only the review rules, no processed data.
